Some Tips for Finding More Inner Peace

Yuhao Zhan · November 8, 2026

To me, one of the deepest meanings of life is to find your inner peace. A peaceful mind means having a sense of clarity, concentration, and gratification in your life, while being at ease with whatever has happened and whatever is going to happen—in other words, being present.

Here are some useful tips distilled from my daily experiences.

1. Exercise

This is especially helpful for people who spend most of their day working in an office.

Exercising for over 30 minutes after a long period of work really clears your mind and recharges you. Aerobic exercise such as running, tennis and swimming works particularly well for me. Sweating through an immersive workout feels like clearing out the mental entropy and waking up a brain that has gone stale after hours of high-density work.

After exercising, the warm shower feels more comfortable, the meal tastes more delicious, and even the sky looks bluer and clearer. It is genuinely one of those small "peak" experiences in my daily life that makes me believe, once again, that life is beautiful.

2. Cut Off the Internet

To put it radically, however, almost 90% of the information on the Internet is nonsense to us—political drama, AI hype, endless trending topics, and countless other things that have little to do with our actual lives. Their main effect is often simply to distract our attention and keep our brains constantly activated.

Your brain is given almost no time to take a break, digest more meaningful content such as the knowledge you have just learned, or make new connections on its own. Instead, it remains constantly overstimulated.

Furthermore, with the popularity of social media and short videos, your attention can become highly fragmented after even a short period of aimless scrolling. Scattered information can be addictive, but it rarely leaves us genuinely relaxed.

Instead, give yourself some leisure time to do NOTHING. Just let your mind be empty. Maybe through meditation, or maybe simply by sitting in front of a window and thinking about nothing in particular. If your thoughts drift away from the present, gently bring them back and continue the "nothing" practice.

After stepping away from the endless stream of Internet information, you may start to see yourself again more clearly, and sometimes new ideas naturally emerge from a surprisingly peaceful mind.

3. Digest Long-Form Content

If you cannot stand doing nothing for a long time and still want some external input, choose long-form content whenever possible.

On the one hand, your mind benefits from steady, logical, and clean information input that it can actually digest. Fragmented content often leaves behind nothing but noise and mental entropy.

On the other hand, high-quality content that lasts for a while—a movie, a deep conversation, a TED Talk, a long thought-provoking article, and so on—can often bring us into a flow state.

In that state, you can genuinely feel that you are learning something, understanding something, or sensing something through an immersive experience. That kind of sustained concentration can gradually quiet the chaos and mess in our minds and help us find inner peace again.

BTW, no AI summaries for these long-form but high-quality articles — they’re worth reading in full.

4. Stop Comparing Yourself to Others

To be honest, most comparisons today come from social media, where everyone seems richer, happier, and more successful than you.

Sometimes you step outside your comfort zone and achieve a new milestone. You feel proud of yourself for a moment—but the glow of that achievement disappears in a flash when you open social media and see someone doing even better in the same area.

However, the fact is that everyone ONLY presents the best version of themselves on social media. All of these curated "best versions" accumulate on your feed and create the illusion that everyone else is living a better life than you.

But this is NOT the truth.

Everyone has their own troubles and problems, most of which are never shared publicly and therefore never seen by you.

Besides, even if someone is ahead of you in one particular dimension, that does NOT mean you are a loser. Everyone has their own talents, baseline, circumstances, and reference frame. You live within your own reference frame.

You only need to compare yourself with YOUR own past self. If you are better than before, that is progress.

Also, if you find it difficult to stop comparing yourself with others, cutting off the Internet, or simply deleting social media apps from your phone, can reduce those comparisons dramatically.

5. Stop Multitasking

The same principle still holds: receiving steady, logical, and clean information while deeply focused can actually reduce the entropy in your brain.

Multitasking means trying to handle several tasks at once.

People often adopt this strategy when they are faced with many urgent deadlines. It feels as if working on multiple things simultaneously should increase efficiency and help clear the queue faster.

The reality, however, is that some residue from the previous task remains in your mind when you move on to the next one.If you constantly switch between tasks, those unfinished thoughts start to overlap. Your brain has to spend extra energy disconnecting from the previous task and rebuilding the context required for the new one.

After enough switching, your mind becomes too messy and tired to process anything properly. You feel frustrated, yet you never experience the gratification that comes from a long period of deeply focused work.

The more tasks you try to do at once, the less peaceful you feel.

6. Be a Long-Termist

This is one of the most effective ways I have found to alleviate anxiety and regain inner peace.

Sometimes you get stuck on one particular thing and simply cannot get out of it. You may fear that one failure will doom your future. You may feel embarrassed about something foolish you did. You may worry that you are falling behind while everyone else seems to be moving forward.

But if you zoom out and look at everything across a much longer time horizon, you may realize that many of those "big" things are not actually big enough to determine your entire life.

Think about the failures and embarrassing moments that happened several years ago. You probably cannot even remember most of them now.

Even though they made you deeply uncomfortable at the time, you kept moving forward, got through those obstacles, and eventually became a new version of yourself.

Now imagine yourself ten years from today.

Will you still feel so stuck and desperate about something that seems unsolvable right now? Will you still be overwhelmed by this one failure, embarrassment, or feeling of falling behind?

Most problems eventually find their solutions—or simply stop being problems at all. On a long enough timeline, many things that feel enormous today become surprisingly small.

7. The Empty Boat Mindset

Imagine that you are sitting quietly in a boat, slowly drifting across the water.

Suddenly, another boat crashes into yours and knocks you off course. You immediately feel annoyed. You assume someone must have been careless, and you are ready to confront them.

But when you turn around, you realize that it is an EMPTY boat. No one is inside. It is simply floating on the water.

Your anger immediately becomes weaker. Instead of complaining, you simply adjust your direction and continue on your way.

Why?

Because it is an empty boat. There is no one to blame. The only thing you can do is focus on yourself, find a solution, and stop giving the disruption any more attention than necessary.

Do you see the magic?

What if you could sometimes treat other people’s behavior toward you like the bump of an empty boat? Whether someone ignores you, misunderstands you, disrespects you, or disappoints you, you do not always have to spend hours wondering:

Why did they treat me like that?

This does not mean tolerating harmful behavior or abandoning your boundaries. It simply means refusing to waste endless emotional energy trying to understand something that you may never fully understand.

Sometimes, instead of solving the mystery of why, you can simply adjust your direction and keep moving. Treat the disruption as an “empty boat.”

8. Let Them Be

There are too many things in this world that you simply cannot control, so spending one more second obsessing over the uncontrollable does not make much sense.

You cannot control what other people think of you, but what you CAN control is how you respond to them.

You cannot control exactly what will happen in the future, but what you CAN control is the present—your devotion, your choices, and your actions.

Focus on the things you can change, even if they seem very small. Every great achievement begins with small steps that accumulate and compound over time.

And for everything you cannot control?

Just let them be.
